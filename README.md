# LED Resistance Experiment

# 1. Introduction
This project investigates how resistance affects current in a simple LED circuit.
I used the Falstad Circuit Simulator to perform the experiment on my Mac.
The aim was to compare theoretical current calculations with simulated values.

# 2. Objective
To study the relationship between resistance and current in a series LED circuit.
To observe how current changes when resistance increases while voltage remains constant.
To compare theoretical estimates with current values obtained through simulation.

# 3. Tools Used
- Mac computer and web browser
- Falstad Circuit Simulator
- Ohm's law and basic electrical theory

# 4. Circuit Simulation
I created a series circuit containing a 5 V DC source, a resistor and an LED.
I tested the circuit using resistance values of 220 Ω, 470 Ω and 1,000 Ω.
[Open my Falstad circuit simulation](https://www.falstad.com/s.py?s=LhRh49)

# 5. Theory
Ohm's law states that current through a resistor is related to voltage and resistance.
For an LED circuit, the approximate current is calculated using I = (Vs − Vf) / R.
Here, Vs is the supply voltage, Vf is the LED forward voltage and R is resistance.

# 6. Method
I set the supply voltage to 5 V and connected the resistor and LED in series.
I changed the resistance to 220 Ω, 470 Ω and 1,000 Ω and recorded the simulated current.
I then compared the observed values with theoretical estimates assuming a 2 V LED forward voltage.

# 7. Discussion
At 220 Ω, the simulated current was 14.5 mA, compared with a theoretical estimate of 13.6 mA.
The differences I observed decreased to approximately 0.5 mA at 470 Ω and 0.3 mA at 1,000 Ω.
This may be due to the simplified assumption of a constant LED forward voltage in my calculations.

# 8. Conclusion
The experiment showed that increasing resistance reduces current in a series LED circuit.
The simulated results generally followed the theoretical prediction, with some differences.
I learned how resistance controls current and how circuit models can differ from simplified calculations.

# 9. Limitations
This experiment used a simulation rather than physical electronic components.
The theoretical calculations assumed an LED forward voltage of approximately 2 V.
Results may vary depending on the LED model and the assumptions used.

# 10. What I Learned
I learned how voltage, resistance and current are related in a simple circuit.
I understood how to apply Ohm's law and compare calculations with simulated results.
I also learned that an LED's forward voltage is not perfectly constant.
